I — ON THE GREY

The Pale

Between every scrap of land there is a grey that has no weather and no year. Sailors call it the Pale. It does not flood, it does not freeze — it only erases, patiently, the way a name wears off a gravestone nobody visits anymore.

No ship has crossed it and come back the same. No signal has crossed it and stayed intact. Every isola left standing is an island in that grey now, and every isola believes, quietly, that it is the last one still lit.

II — WHAT REMAINS

Isolas

The isolas trade a little less with each passing decade — not from spite, but from distance. The grey between them widens a few meters every year, unhurried, undramatic, and the maps are updated less often than they should be, because nobody wants to be the cartographer who admits the world is shrinking.

Somewhere on one of them a mural is fading from a century that already failed. Somewhere on another, someone is repainting it from memory, badly, and calling that hope.

III — THE HISS

Static

Underneath the noise there is supposedly a frequency that still carries a voice. Nobody agrees on what it says. Some hear a folk song from a country that doesn't exist anymore. Some hear a shipping forecast. Some hear a woman laughing. Most hear nothing, and keep listening anyway.

IV — STILL TURNING

A World, Unfinished

A world doesn't need saving to keep turning. It only needs someone, somewhere, still fool enough to tune in — and to sit with the hiss until it starts, almost, to sound like company.
